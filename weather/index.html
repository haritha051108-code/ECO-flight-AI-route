About Persistent Contrails & Climate Impact

What are persistent contrails? When aircraft fly through very cold and humid regions of the upper atmosphere, water vapor and soot from engines condense into ice crystals.

In Ice-Supersaturated Regions (ISSR), these contrails do not evaporate. Instead, they expand into man-made cirrus cloud sheets that trap heat emitted by the Earth, contributing significantly to aviation's net climate warming.

How EcoFlight AI works:
We query live atmospheric telemetry (temperature and relative humidity at cruising altitudes between 250–300 hPa) from global meteorological models. Using the Schmidt-Appleman Criterion, we identify ice-supersaturated risk zones and calculate small tactical route or altitude adjustments that eliminate persistent contrail formation with minimal fuel and time trade-offs.

Risk Color Coding:
● Green (Low): Dry or warm air where contrails evaporate immediately.
● Yellow (Medium): Marginal ice-saturation with transient contrails.
● Red (High): Cold ISSR zone where persistent warming cirrus will form.
